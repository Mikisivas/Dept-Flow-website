Nine unauthenticated routes, and the only two places the full crest appears

A student's first contact with the system is a link pasted into a class WhatsApp group, opened on a phone, on cellular data. Nothing here assumes they know what Dept-Flow is.

Landing

Answers one question — what is this and why am I on it — then gets out of the way. Three cards, two buttons, no marketing.

Notes

  • The checkpoint pair appears in the first card, so the motif is taught before it is used.
  • The privacy sentence sits in the footer of the first page, not buried behind a link — a location prompt with no warning is what makes students refuse it.
  • Droppable: if the URL should open straight on login, this page goes and / redirects.

Routes

Matric check, forgot and reset

One field each. The matric check exists so a student discovers an impostor early, in September, rather than at the eligibility list in March.

Routes

/privacy · /404 · /500

Privacy notice and error pages

The privacy notice is a page students will actually be pointed at during registration, so it is written for them rather than for a regulator.

Notes

  • Error pages carry the simplified mark, not the crest — at this scale the crest is decoration, and the rule reserves it for landing, login and print.
  • The 500 page names the attendance consequence, because a student hitting it mid-checkpoint is the one person who must act immediately.
  • Every field is 48px tall with a 16px font — under 16px, iOS Safari zooms the viewport on focus.
  • Matric numbers and course codes carry translate="no" and spellcheck="false" throughout.
